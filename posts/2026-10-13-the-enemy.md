The animation system was working, but only on a test tube. A tube can prove the maths; it cannot prove that a character will look right and behave in a corridor. The same afternoon, I built the real thing: an enemy, made in Blender from CC0 sources only, and then placed it in the hallway to see whether it could do its job.

## Authoring the enemy

I needed a human body with a proper skeleton, and I did not want to model and rig one from nothing. I approved one new tool: MPFB 2, a Blender extension from the official extensions site (I checked the download against the published SHA-256 hash). Its code is GPL, so it stays a tool and nothing of it ships in the engine. The MakeHuman base mesh and `game_engine` rig it outputs are CC0. I did not download its optional pack of clothes and hair.

The key choice was to author the whole enemy from a script, `build_enemy.py`, run headless in Blender. That means I can rerun it and tune it, instead of making one-off edits by hand that I cannot reproduce.

- **Body:** the MPFB human shaped gaunt: older, thin, wasted muscle, hollow cheeks, a long neck, 1.87 m tall. Helper geometry is removed and the mesh is reduced to the triangle budget. Small dark eyes sit deep in the sockets.
- **Rig:** MPFB's skeleton, 53 deform bones with the root at the feet, using its skin weights cut down to four per vertex.
- **Rags:** cut from the body's own faces, so they bend with it. They are pushed out 1.5 to 4 cm, with folds, torn along a noisy hem and worn through in holes.
- **Materials:** two, both matte, which matters after the roughness lesson from earlier in the week. Skin is roughness 0.9, pallid, mottled and bruised, darker and dirtier at the hands and feet. Rags are 0.95, grey-brown cloth with old stains. The albedo textures are baked in Cycles from procedural noise, 2048 for the skin and 1024 for the rags.

> **Concept: Baking a texture.** A procedural material is a recipe, "noise, then threshold, then blend", that computes a colour for any point. A game cannot run that recipe per pixel, so you *bake* it: render the recipe once onto the mesh's UV layout and save the result as an ordinary image. The engine then just samples an image.

### The three clips

The animations are keyed in code from one hunched stance, so they share a personality.

- **Idle** (4 s): two heavy breaths, a weight shift, and one sharp twitch of the head.
- **Walk** (2 s): a dragging shamble. The right leg is short and scrapes, and the root moves 1.1 m per cycle.
- **Lurch** (1.6 s, does not loop): a recoil, then a lunge with both arms reaching and the head up, trembling, then a half slump. The root moves 0.7 m.

The sidecar marks the sound-worthy moments: `footstep_left` at 0.5 s and `footstep_right` at 1.5 s of Walk, and `lunge` at 0.37 s of Lurch. No sounds exist yet. The markers are ready for them.

### Exporter and checker upgrades

The Blender exporter got a skinned mode: deform bones only, the four strongest influences, each action as a clip, sampled every frame from the rest pose. It warns about no armature or more than one, a mesh without the Armature modifier, vertices no bone moves, more than four weights, and actions that would be lost on save. The glTF checker gained character budgets (25k triangles, 64 joints, 2 materials) and checks for one skin, at least one clip and no duplicate clip names.

The engine's own import reports 23,406 triangles, 53 joints, 2 materials and 3 clips, with root motion of -1.1 m for Walk and -0.7 m for Lurch along the engine's forward axis, which is the -Z that the mirrored glTF axis gives me. Rendered through the viewer's skin-weight mode, the thigh weights are clean.

![The three clips, thigh weights and skeleton](/images/2026-10-13-the-enemy/sheet.jpg "Idle, Walk and Lurch in the dim rig, then the weights on the left thigh, then Walk with bones drawn.")

Not done yet: the skin and cloth have no normal map, so they read flat up close. Feet slide a little in Walk, because the root moves evenly while steps do not. Fingers stay in the rest pose, and the eyes share the rags material to stay inside two materials.

## The enemy in the hallway

The character entity in the north corridor has four parts: the mesh, an Animator (Idle, with root motion on), a capsule collider 0.3 m in radius and 1.75 m tall, so the character controller stops it at walls and holds it to the floor, and a script called `EnemyPatrol`.

![The enemy walking down the north corridor](/images/2026-10-13-the-enemy/patrol.jpg "A figure at the far end of the north corridor, barely lit, walking toward the camera.")

### Patrol

The script walks the enemy between entities tagged as waypoints, in name order, standing for four seconds at each. Idle and Walk crossfade over half a second. It turns toward the next waypoint at 70 degrees a second, and sets off only once it is roughly facing it, so it never slides sideways. The Walk clip's root motion carries it forward. If it makes no progress for 2.5 s, it warns and gives up on that waypoint.

Other scripts can call `Ambush(mark, toward, delay, hold)`: it puts the enemy at a mark, facing a point (the player), standing still. After the delay it plays Lurch, stands for the hold time, then goes back to its patrol. Footfall and lunge events call a sound property if one is set, so audio can plug in later.

### The closet scare

The scare script that already turned the lights out when the player entered the closet trigger now has two more fields. Entering the trigger still kills the lights. 0.3 s later, the enemy is standing at a mark up the east corridor, facing the player. 1.1 s after that it lurches: a recoil, a lunge of 0.7 m, then a slump. Then it walks off along its patrol.

![The scare sequence](/images/2026-10-13-the-enemy/scare.jpg "It appears up the east corridor, recoils, lunges, slumps, and walks away.")

### Engine fixes the scene needed

- **Teleporting a character.** A script setting a root-motion character's position now moves its capsule too. Before, the next step pulled it straight back.
- **Baking.** Skinned meshes are left out of the bake geometry. A character moves, so baking its bind pose into probes and lightmaps would leave its shadow where it first stood.
- **Editor stutter.** Every edit rebuilds the render view and its edit-mode poses, and each rebuild loaded the enemy from the disk cache again, about 20 ms in Debug. The cache only shares a mesh while something holds it, and animators were not holding theirs. They now keep the skinned meshes they bind. Separately, the bake staleness check was computing the scene's probe key three times after each edit; it computes it once. The Debug smoothness check went from 30 hitches to 0 to 2.

## Verifying it

A new test runs the saved hallway scene in a real game session with the game's own C# scripts. Over 80 seconds, the enemy reaches waypoint 1, then 2, then 1 again, stays inside the corridor (so the walls stop its capsule), stays on the floor, and lands about one footfall per second of walking. For the scare, it appears at the mark at 0.32 s, lurches at 1.45 s, and the lunge event fires at 1.82 s. It closes from 4.0 m to 3.3 m without reaching the player, then goes back to patrolling.

I also looked at the motion-vector debug view while it walks. The vectors appear on the enemy only, the still scene is black, and there is no TAA trail or ghosting on its edges.

At 1440p, the enemy costs at most 0.06 ms of GPU time, at the pose where it is in view (3.51 to 3.57 ms), well inside my 0.5 ms budget. Its shadow work reports separately at 0.00 to 0.04 ms, since the posed enemy re-renders the shadow tiles of the lamps that see it.

## Where it ended up

An enemy now lives in the north corridor and takes part in the closet scare. What is still wanted: footstep and lunge sounds, normal maps, planted feet, and a closer lunge if the scare needs more teeth. The patrol is a straight line between waypoints, so corners need a waypoint at each turn, or navigation later. Moving the enemy in the editor still marks the bakes stale even though it is not baked, which needs one more rebake to fix.
