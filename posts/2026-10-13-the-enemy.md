The animation system worked, but only on a test tube. A tube can prove the arithmetic of skinning. It cannot show whether a character looks right in a dark corridor or whether the scripts and the engine hold up when it walks. The same afternoon I authored a real character in Blender, from CC0 sources only, and placed it in the hallway to see whether it could do the job.

## Authoring the enemy

### The tool and the licence

I needed a human body with a proper skeleton and skin weights, and I did not want to model and rig one from nothing. I approved one new tool, MPFB 2.0.18, a Blender extension from the official extensions site. I checked the 43.8 MB download against the published SHA-256 hash. The extension's code is GPL, so it remains a tool and nothing of it ships in the engine. The MakeHuman base mesh and the `game_engine` rig it produces are CC0. I did not download its optional pack of clothes and hair.

I author the whole enemy from a script, `build_enemy.py`, run headless in Blender. I can rerun it and tune a parameter, which I cannot do with one-off edits made by hand. The source `.blend` is stored with Git LFS.

### How a game character is built

A character for a real-time engine has four parts that must agree with each other: a mesh at a triangle budget, a skeleton, skin weights that bind one to the other, and materials. These are the choices I made for each.

**The body.** The MPFB human is shaped gaunt: older, thin, with wasted muscle, hollow cheeks and a long neck, 1.87 m tall. The helper geometry MPFB adds is removed and the mesh is decimated to the budget. The engine reports 23,406 triangles against a character budget of 25,000. Small dark eyes sit deep in the sockets.

**The rig.** MPFB's `game_engine` skeleton has 53 deform bones with the root at the feet. Its skin weights are cut down to four per vertex, which is the engine's limit. A vertex then follows its four strongest joints and the weights add up to 1. I checked the result in the skin-weight view. The weights on the left thigh are clean, with the thigh bone's influence fading across the hip and the knee instead of breaking at a hard edge.

**The rags.** The clothing is cut from the body's own faces. It therefore bends with the body, and the cloth does not need a separate skinning setup. I push the faces out 1.5 to 4 cm, add folds, tear the hem along a noisy line and wear holes in it.

**Materials.** There are two, and both are matte. The skin has roughness 0.9, a pallid, mottled and bruised albedo, and is darker and dirtier at the hands and feet. The rags have roughness 0.95 and a grey-brown cloth with old stains. The albedo textures are baked in Cycles from procedural noise, 2048 pixels for the skin and 1024 for the rags.

> **Background: baking a texture.** A procedural material is a recipe, such as "noise, then threshold, then blend", that can compute a colour for any point of a surface. A game cannot run that recipe for every pixel of every frame. Baking runs it once. For each texel of the mesh's UV layout, the baker finds the point on the surface that the texel maps to, evaluates the recipe there and stores the colour in an image. The engine then only samples an ordinary image. The cost moves to authoring time, and the result depends on the UV layout having no overlaps, which a bake needs so that each texel belongs to exactly one place on the surface.

### The three clips

I key the animations in code from one hunched stance, so the three share a posture and personality.

| Clip | Length | Loops | Content | Root travel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idle | 4 s | yes | Two heavy breaths, a weight shift and one sharp twitch of the head | none |
| Walk | 2 s | yes | A dragging shamble. The right leg is short and scrapes. | 1.1 m a cycle |
| Lurch | 1.6 s | no | A recoil, then a lunge with both arms reaching and the head up, trembling, then a half slump | 0.7 m |

The sidecar marks events for later audio: `footstep_left` at 0.5 s and `footstep_right` at 1.5 s of Walk, and `lunge` at 0.37 s of Lurch. No sounds exist yet, and the markers are ready for them.

### Exporter and checker changes

The Blender exporter has a skinned mode. It exports deform bones only, with the four strongest influences, starts each action from the rest pose and samples every frame. Sampling every frame matters because the engine resamples to 30 frames a second, and a curve that Blender evaluates between keys must be sampled before it leaves Blender. The exporter warns about no armature or more than one, a mesh without the Armature modifier, vertices that no bone moves, more than four weights, and actions that would be lost on save.

The glTF checker gained character budgets of 25,000 triangles, 64 joints and 2 materials, and checks for exactly one skin, at least one clip and no duplicate clip names.

The engine's own import reports 23,406 triangles, 53 joints, 2 materials and 3 clips. Walk and Lurch have root motion of -1.1 m and -0.7 m along the engine's forward axis, which is the -Z that the mirrored glTF axis gives me.

![The three clips, thigh weights and skeleton](/images/2026-10-13-the-enemy/sheet.jpg "Idle, Walk and Lurch in the dim rig, then the weights on the left thigh, then Walk with the bones drawn.")

The limits of this first version are the skin and cloth, which have no normal map and read flat up close. The feet slide a little in Walk, because the root moves evenly while the steps do not. The fingers stay in the rest pose, and the eyes share the rags material to stay inside two materials.

## The enemy in the hallway

The character entity in the north corridor has four parts: the mesh, an Animator on Idle with root motion on, a capsule collider 0.3 m in radius and 1.75 m tall so the character controller stops it at walls and holds it to the floor, and a script called `EnemyPatrol`.

![The enemy in the hallway in edit mode](/images/2026-10-13-the-enemy/hallway.jpg "The enemy placed in the hallway in edit mode, showing the first frame of Idle under the real lights.")

### A patrol as a state machine

A state machine describes behaviour as a set of states, one of which is active, and a set of transitions that each leave one state for another when a condition holds. It suits a character because the animation, the movement and the timers all depend on a single "what is it doing now" value, and the machine makes that value explicit. My animator does not contain a state machine. It plays a clip and crossfades on request, as I described the day before, and the script owns the decisions.

`EnemyPatrol` has three states:

| State | Clip | Leaves when |
|---|---|---|
| Waiting | Idle | the timer has run out and the enemy faces its next waypoint to within 25 degrees, then it goes to Walking |
| Walking | Walk | it is within 0.45 m of the waypoint, then Waiting, or it has made no progress for 2.5 s, then it gives up on that waypoint and moves to the next |
| Ambush | Idle, then Lurch | a timer started by `Ambush(...)` has run out, then Waiting |

It walks between the entities tagged as waypoints, taken in name order, and stands for four seconds at each. Idle and Walk crossfade over half a second.

The turn is a clamped step. Each update it computes the angle error to the target direction and turns by `clamp(error, -step, step)`, where the step is 70 degrees a second times the frame time. It sets off only when the remaining error is under 25 degrees. This matters because the Walk clip's root motion moves the entity along its own forward axis. If the enemy started walking while still facing sideways, it would slide sideways to the waypoint.

Other scripts can call `Ambush(mark, toward, delay, hold)`. It puts the enemy at a mark, facing a point such as the player, standing still. After the delay it plays Lurch once, with looping turned off, because a second lunge would carry it further toward the player. After the hold time it goes back to patrolling. Each call increments a counter that its timers check, so a timer left over from an earlier ambush does nothing. Footfall and lunge events play a sound property if one is set, so audio can plug in later.

![The enemy walking down the north corridor](/images/2026-10-13-the-enemy/patrol.jpg "A figure at the far end of the north corridor, barely lit, walking toward the camera.")

### The closet scare

The scare script that already turned the lights out when the player entered the closet trigger gained two fields. Entering the trigger still kills the lights. 0.3 s later the enemy is standing at a mark up the east corridor, facing the player. 1.1 s after that it lurches, with a recoil, a lunge of 0.7 m and a slump. Then it walks off along its patrol.

![The scare sequence](/images/2026-10-13-the-enemy/scare.jpg "The enemy appears up the east corridor, recoils, lunges, slumps and walks away.")

### Engine changes the scene needed

**Teleporting a character.** When a script set the position of a root-motion character, the capsule stayed behind, and on the next step the controller pulled the character straight back. The script's position change now moves the capsule too.

**Baking.** Lightmaps and probes bake the static scene's light. A character moves, so its bind pose baked into them would leave a shadow where the character first stood. Skinned meshes are left out of the bake geometry, and the character is lit by probes and dynamic lights. A posed character also re-renders the shadow tiles of the lamps that see it.

**Editor stutter.** Every edit rebuilds the render view and its edit-mode poses, and each rebuild loaded the enemy from the disk cache again, about 20 ms in Debug. The mesh cache shares a mesh only while something holds it, and the renderer keeps only the GPU copy, so nothing held the CPU copy. Animators now hold the skinned meshes they bind. Separately, the staleness check for the bakes was computing the scene's probe key three times after each edit, and it now computes it once. The Debug smoothness check went from 30 hitches to between 0 and 2.

## Verifying it

A new test runs the saved hallway scene in a real game session with the game's own C# scripts. Over 80 seconds the enemy reaches waypoint 1, then 2, then 1 again. It stays inside the corridor, which shows the walls stop its capsule, and it stays on the floor. It walks for 64 of the 80 seconds and lands about one footfall per second of walking. In the scare test, the enemy appears at the mark at 0.32 s, lurches at 1.45 s and the lunge event fires at 1.82 s. It closes from 4.0 m to 3.3 m without reaching the player, then returns to patrolling.

I also looked at the motion-vector debug view while it walks. The vectors appear on the enemy only, the still scene is black, and there is no TAA trail or ghosting on its edges.

At 1440p the enemy costs at most 0.06 ms of GPU time, at the pose where it is in view (3.51 to 3.57 ms), inside my 0.5 ms budget for a character. Its shadow work is reported separately at 0.00 to 0.04 ms. All 97 test suites pass in Release and Debug.

## Results and open issues

An enemy now lives in the north corridor and takes part in the closet scare. What is missing:

- footstep and lunge sounds;
- normal maps for the skin and cloth;
- planted feet in Walk, with foot IK or a stepped root curve;
- a closer lunge, if the scare needs more weight;
- corners: the patrol is a straight line between waypoints, so each turn needs a waypoint, or a navigation path later;
- moving the enemy in the editor still marks the bakes stale, although it is not baked. Fixing that needs one more rebake.
